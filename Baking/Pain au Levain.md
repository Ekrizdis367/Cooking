## Ingredients
#### Preferment

- [ ] 28g Sourdough Culture #Baking
- [ ] 150g All-Purpose Flour #Baking
- [ ] 76g Cold Water #Pantry
#### Dough

- [ ] 539g Warm Water #Pantry
- [ ] 630g All-Purpose Flour #Baking
- [ ] 53g Whole Wheat Flour #Baking
- [ ] 18g Fine Salt #Seasoning
## Directions

### Day 1

#### Make the Preferment

1. The evening before you want to bake, in a large bowl, combine the **sourdough culture**, **flour**, and **water**. 
2. Mix until shaggy dough forms. 
3. Transfer the dough to a clean work surface and knead until no dry patches of flour remain and a stiff, smooth dough forms. 
4. Return the dough to the bowl, cover, and let stand overnight at room temperature.

---
### Day 2
#### Make the Dough

1. Add the warm water to the preferment, then squeeze the preferment through your fingers to break it up. 
2. Holding your hand in a clawlike position, vigorously mix the preferment into the water until well combined. 
3. The mixture will look like thin pancake batter with a few small lumps. 
4. Add the **All-Purpose Flour**, **Whole Wheat Flour**, and **Salt** to the bowl.
5. Mix until no dry patches of flour remain and a soft, sticky dough forms.

> [!info] Bulk fermentation or the "first rise" will take 2 1/2 hours. During that time you'll attend to the dough twice, folding the dough first after 30 minutes and again after 1 1/2 hours. Then the dough will rest, covered and undisturbed, for the final hour.

#### Bulk Ferment the Dough

1. Once you've mixed the dough, cover and let rest for 30 minutes. 
2. With a wet hand, perform 10-12 bowl folds until the dough begins to resist being pulled and smooths out. 
3. Cover and let rest at room temperature for 60 minutes. 
4. With a wet hand, perform 10-12 more bowl folds until the dough begins to resist stretching. 
5. Cover and let rise another 60 minutes.

#### Pre-shape the Dough

1. Lightly flour a work surface, then use a plastic bowl scraper to ease the dough out of the bowl onto the work surface. 
2. Gently deflate the dough and divide into 2 equals portions. 
3. Pre-shape each piece into a round, then place the rounds seam side up on a floured surface. 
4. Cover and let rest for 20 minutes.

#### Shape the Dough & Let it Rise

1. Generously dust proofing baskets with floor. 
2. After the 20-minute rest, shape each piece of dough into a boule or Batard, then place them seam side up in the prepared baskets. 

> [!note] At this point, you can also refrigerate the shaped loaves overnight, which will give them a deeper, tangier flavor. Let the refrigerated loaves rise at room temperature for 1 hour before baking.

3. Cover and let rise until the dough feels light and almost marshmallow, and when pressed with a floured finger, a small impression remains.

---
### Covered Baker

#### Preheat

1. One hour before the loaves are finished rising, arrange a rack in the lower third of the oven and preheat the oven to 450F, with the lidded baker set on the lower rack.
2. Turn one loaf out of its proofing basket onto a round of parchment paper with a sling. 
3. Keep the second loaf refrigerated while you bake the first. 
4. Use a baker's lame or razor blade to score the top of the loaf.

> [!important] It is important to score the loaf so that it can properly rise further.
#### Bake

1. Remove the covered baker from the oven, uncover, and - using the parchment paper sling - carefully lower the loaf into the pan. 
2. Place the lid on the baker, return to the oven, and bake for 20 minutes.
3. Uncover and continue baking until the bread is deep and golden brown and the internal temperature reaches at least 200F - 15-20 minutes more.
4. Remove the pan from the oven, carefully lift out the loaf, and transfer to a rack to cool completely. 
5. Return the lidded baker to the oven and let heat for 30 minutes, then score and bake the second loaf as directed above. 
6. Let the bread cool completely before slicing.
